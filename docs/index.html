01 — The problem

Your inbox is full of mail you asked for in 2019.

Every free trial, checkout and webinar signed you up for something. Gmail files them under Promotions and calls it handled — but they still pile up, still bury the mail that matters, and a surprising number of them are subscriptions you're paying for.

Unsubscribing one at a time means hunting for a grey link at the bottom of each email. Manila reads the Gmail page you already have open, groups every sender, and turns the whole thing into one list you can act on.

04 — Questions

Things people ask us.

Only on your screen. It's a browser extension that works on the Gmail tab you already have open — it reads sender names, subjects and dates from your inbox view, never the body of your mail. No server, no Gmail API, no Google sign-in. Nothing is uploaded.

Manila opens the sender's own unsubscribe page, or their most recent email, so you can confirm — that's the request senders are legally bound to honour. Pro users can select several subscriptions and unsubscribe from all of them in one go.

It hides that sender inside Manila Mail Manager: they drop out of your subscription list, stop being counted in your stats, and lose their Gmail badge. Nothing changes about your actual subscription — their emails keep arriving in Gmail as before.

It's a one-click tidy: every subscription email in the list you're looking at folds out of view, leaving the mail from real people. It's a view filter and nothing more — those emails stay exactly where they are, and one more click brings them back.

Yes. Cancel from the extension's settings or through the receipt Paddle emails you. You keep Pro until the end of the period you've paid for, then drop back to Free — nothing is deleted.

Three. A Chrome profile counts as one device, so a work laptop, a home machine and a second profile all fit. If you run out — say after a reinstall or a new laptop — open Settings in the extension and deactivate a device you no longer use.

Only occasionally. Activating Pro checks in with our licensing service, and the extension re-checks quietly in the background. If it can't reach us — offline, or a firewall in the way — Pro keeps working for a grace period rather than switching off.
